Strengthening Cyber Resilience 
 
The Insurance Authority (“IA”) reminds authorized insurers, licensed insurance broker 
companies and licensed insurance agencies to remain vigilant in managing cyber risk 
and ensuring operational resilience.  
  
Recent incidents, including a cyberattack involving unauthorised access to and leakage 
of personal information, and a major global technology outage affecting widely used 
systems and services, suggest that business entities may be affected by both malicious 
attacks and disruptions through shared services, group arrangements, and other 
dependency linkages.  In light of these incidents, insurers, insurance broker companies 
and insurance agencies should review the adequacy and effectiveness of your internal 
system controls having regard to relevant IA guidelines1 or circulars2.  Among other 
things, you should ensure that your –  
 
(a) cyber controls remain appropriate to current threats and consistent with your 
use of technology and third-party services; 
 
(b) understanding of critical business services and their end-to-end dependencies is 
up-to-date; 
 
(c) incident response and recovery arrangements remain practical and executable; 
 
(d) communication with customers and stakeholders is timely, accurate and 
coordinated, avoiding actions or messages that could increase risk or 
operational strain;

(e) arrangements to support the availability and integrity of systems and data are 
robust, and that backup and recovery arrangements remain commensurate with 
operational needs and risk profile; and 
 
(f) incident response drills and penetration tests are conducted regularly to 
strengthen preparedness for cybersecurity threats and to help identify 
vulnerabilities in systems and controls. 
 
The IA also reminds insurers, insurance broker companies and insurance agencies to 
make appropriate use of the Cyber Intelligence Sharing Platform (“CISP 3 ”), as 
applicable, to monitor advisories, share timely and actionable threat intelligence.
